Explicit simulator-level reset perturbation on real Isaac Lab captures. It is modeled after a reset-semantics failure observed while developing the parity harness and fixed in upstream commit 9aaa389. It is not a literal reproduction of that historical case: this adapter writes zero cart-pole joint velocity instead of the requested 0.5 rad/s, while the historical harness omitted a rigid-body root velocity write. The bundle records the deliberate switch. IVF validity and oracle decisions do not consume that bookkeeping field; they establish the trajectory and event consequences.

pole_angle_agreement tolerance rationale: The baseline pole crosses 1.0 rad at a measured median rate of 3.456 rad/s. At 120 Hz, the local one-period angular displacement is 3.456 rad/s / 120 Hz = 0.0288 rad. This engineering budget corresponds to one control period near that crossing. It does not guarantee that a sub-budget difference cannot change the sampled crossing index; the event oracle checks timing directly.
scope: per-step absolute pole-angle error, reduced over environments
pole_velocity_agreement tolerance rationale: Capture index 0 is the first post-reset step. The baseline median threshold index is 37, so median elapsed time is 38 / 120 = 0.3166667 s. Requiring a sustained pole-rate difference to accumulate no more than the 0.0288 rad angle budget over that measured median time gives 0.0288 rad / 0.3166667 s = 0.0909474 rad/s. This oracle exists because of what it observes, not only what it gates: a reset that drops velocity leaves position identical at step 0 and only diverges through integration, so a position-only manifest sees a smooth growing error and classifies it as drift. Watching the velocity is what makes the step-0 signature visible and the divergence classifiable.
scope: per-step absolute pole angular-rate error, reduced over environments

mean_pole_angle_equivalence tolerance rationale: Half the one-control-step angle budget above: 0.5 * 0.0288 = 0.0144 rad. The horizon-mean margin is deliberately tighter than the pointwise allowance so a persistent shift is held to a stronger criterion than a transient difference.
